# Marketing Playbooks

Channel-specific playbooks for Phase 3. Load when building the marketing & sales plan.

---

## Playbook 1: Community Seeding

**Best for:** Niche products, B2C, tools for specific professions or hobbies
**Time to first customer:** 1–2 weeks
**Budget required:** $0

**Steps:**
1. Identify 3–5 communities where your exact customer hangs out (subreddits, Discord servers, Facebook Groups, Slack communities, forums)
2. Spend 1 week being genuinely helpful — answer questions, share value, build credibility. Do NOT pitch.
3. In week 2, share your thing in context: "I built this because I kept seeing this problem here..."
4. DM the 5–10 most engaged people who respond. Ask for feedback, not sales.
5. Turn early responders into founding members with a special offer or early access.

**Common mistake:** Posting a promotional link in a community you just joined. You'll get banned and burned.

---

## Playbook 2: Cold Outreach (DMs / Email)

**Best for:** B2B, productized services, high-ticket offers
**Time to first customer:** 3–10 days
**Budget required:** $0

**The formula:**
```
Line 1: Hyper-specific compliment or observation (not "I love your work")
Line 2: The problem you think they have (one sentence)
Line 3: What you've built or can do about it
Line 4: The ask (one specific, low-stakes action)
```

**Example:**
> "Saw your post about struggling to get consistent content out — you mentioned it's the writing part that slows you down. I built a tool that turns your voice notes into ready-to-post LinkedIn drafts. Want me to run one of your voice notes through it for free?"

**Volume:** Send 20–30 highly targeted messages before judging results. Don't blast 200 generic ones.

**Platforms by audience:**
- Founders: Twitter DMs, LinkedIn
- Creators: Instagram DMs, YouTube comments
- Professionals: LinkedIn
- Niche hobbyists: Discord, Reddit DMs, niche forums

## Playbook 3: Content Loop

**Best for:** Creator businesses, B2C products with education angle, building long-term audiences
**Time to first customer:** 2–6 weeks (slower but compounds)
**Budget required:** $0

**The loop:**
1. Pick one platform where your customer already spends time
2. Post about the *problem* your product solves — not the product itself
3. In every piece of content, have one clear next step (follow, DM, link in bio)
4. Once you have 100 followers who engage, make an offer

**Content types that convert:**
- "Here's what I learned doing X" (credibility)
- "The reason most people fail at X" (identifies the problem)
- "I tried X for 30 days, here's what happened" (story + proof)
- "Unpopular opinion about X" (contrarian angle drives discussion)

**Platform fit guide:**
- Short-form video (TikTok, Reels, YouTube Shorts): lifestyle, physical products, consumer tools
- LinkedIn: B2B, professional services, career tools
- Twitter/X: tech, founder tools, thought leadership
- Instagram: physical products, aesthetics, wellness, fashion
- YouTube: tutorials, in-depth reviews, education products
- Newsletter: any niche with an information gap

## Playbook 4: Waitlist Launch

**Best for:** New products not yet built, validating demand before building
**Time to first customer:** 2–4 weeks (validation), longer to revenue
**Budget required:** $0–$100

**Steps:**
1. Build a one-page landing page (Carrd, Framer, or Notion) — headline, 3 bullet points, email signup
2. Drive traffic via community seeding or cold outreach (see above)
3. Email every signup within 24 hours — personally. Ask what made them sign up.
4. Set a threshold: "If I get 50 signups in 2 weeks, I'll build this." If not, pivot.
5. For paid waitlists: offer founding member pricing ($X now to lock in) — this is the real validation

**Waitlist page formula:**
- Headline: The outcome, not the product ("Never spend Sunday meal prepping again")
- Subheadline: Who it's for and how it works (one sentence)
- 3 bullets: The 3 things they're most afraid of / frustrated by, solved
- Social proof: Even "Join 47 people on the waitlist" works
- CTA: "Get early access" not "Sign up"

## Playbook 5: Product Hunt Launch

**Best for:** Software products, tools for makers/founders/developers
**Time to prepare:** 2–4 weeks before launch
**Budget required:** $0

**What actually matters:**
1. **Hunter:** Ask a well-known PH user to hunt you (DM people who hunted similar products)
2. **Launch day traffic:** Your own network drives early upvotes — email list, Slack, Discord, Twitter
3. **Timing:** Launch Tuesday–Thursday, 12:01am PST
4. **Gallery:** First screenshot should show the product in action, not a logo
5. **Comment back:** Respond to every comment on launch day — shows engagement

**What PH is NOT:** It's not a customer acquisition channel by itself. It's a credibility signal and PR moment. Use it to get into maker media, newsletters, and communities.

## Playbook 6: Cross-Border Launch

**Best for:** Products with geographic arbitrage, physical goods, cultural products
**Time to first customer:** Varies by product type
**Budget required:** Low to moderate

**Channels by product type:**

*Physical product from Country A → Country B:*
1. Find diaspora communities in Country B (Facebook Groups, Reddit, local associations)
2. Sell to the diaspora first — they already know and want the product
3. Use their testimonials to market to mainstream consumers as "authentic" or "imported"

*Knowledge arbitrage (info known in one country, not another):*
1. Find the communities in Country B asking questions that Country A has already solved
2. Create educational content (YouTube, newsletter, course) bridging the knowledge gap
3. Monetize via course, consulting, or community membership

*Service arbitrage (quality service at lower price point):*
1. Build credibility on Upwork, Toptal, or niche platforms
2. Target clients in high-cost markets (US, UK, Australia, UAE)
3. Deliver at Western quality, price below Western rates

## Playbook 7: The Founder Story

**Best for:** Any product — the founder's story is always the most shareable marketing asset

**The formula:**
1. **Before:** What was your life/problem like before you built this?
2. **Moment:** What specific thing happened that made you decide to build it?
3. **Build:** What did you sacrifice or struggle with while building it?
4. **Result:** What changed for you? What changed for early users?
5. **Invitation:** Why should this person join your journey?

**Where to tell it:**
- Launch post (Reddit, IndieHackers, LinkedIn, Twitter)
- About page of your website
- First email to your waitlist
- Podcast appearances
- Cold outreach intro

**The rule:** People don't share products. They share stories. Make your story shareable.
