# Idea Frameworks

Deep frameworks for idea generation. Load when base Phase 2 isn't producing sharp enough ideas.

---

## 1. Jobs To Be Done (JTBD) Filter

Every product is hired to do a job. Ask:

- **Functional job:** What task does it literally help complete?
- **Emotional job:** How does the customer want to feel after using it?
- **Social job:** How does it change how others perceive the customer?

The best ideas solve all three. Example: Expensive gym memberships aren't bought for fitness (functional) — they're bought to feel disciplined (emotional) and signal health to peers (social).

**Application:** For each person's interest or skill, ask "What job is being done when someone pays for this?"

---

## 2. Cross-Border Arbitrage Checklist

Products that work in one geography often haven't crossed borders yet. Check:

- **Category 1 — Untranslated Markets:** Product exists in Country A, nobody has brought it to Country B. (e.g., Korean skincare in Latin America in 2015; bubble tea in Eastern Europe in 2020)
- **Category 2 — Price Arbitrage:** Something is 10x cheaper to produce in one country and can be sold at a premium elsewhere.
- **Category 3 — Cultural Export:** A food, practice, tool, or tradition from one culture has high perceived value in another.
- **Category 4 — Regulatory Arbitrage:** A service legal in one country, prohibited or restricted in another — and people are willing to pay for access.
- **Category 5 — Knowledge Arbitrage:** Information that is common knowledge in one country is unknown or inaccessible in another.

**Signals to look for in the intake:**
- Person has lived in multiple countries
- Person speaks multiple languages
- Person has strong ties to a diaspora community
- Person mentions a product they "can't find here"

---

## 3. Blue Ocean Filter

Instead of competing in an existing market, find white space.

Four actions:
1. **Eliminate** — What do all competitors do that customers don't actually care about?
2. **Reduce** — What features or costs could be dramatically cut?
3. **Raise** — What does the market underdeliver on that customers desperately want?
4. **Create** — What does no competitor offer that would create new demand?

Ask the person: "What's the thing everyone in this market does that you find stupid or unnecessary?"

---

## 4. Job Adjacency Mapping

Map what the person is already good at → what jobs are adjacent → what products serve those jobs.

Example:
```
Person is good at: Excel / financial modeling
Adjacent jobs:
- Small business owners who need financial clarity but can't afford a CFO
- Freelancers who don't understand their own pricing
- Students applying to MBA programs who need to build models

Products:
- Productized financial review service ($299/month)
- Pricing calculator tool for freelancers
- MBA financial modeling course
```

## 5. Complaint Mining Framework

The fastest path to product ideas: find where people complain online.

Sources:
- Reddit: search "[niche] + frustrated / annoying / wish / hate / alternative"
- Amazon reviews: 3-star reviews reveal what's almost good but missing
- App Store reviews: same principle for software
- Twitter/X: "[industry] + nobody / someone should / why isn't there"
- Facebook Groups: read the pinned posts and recurring questions
- Glassdoor: complaints about tools employees are forced to use

**Apply to the person's domain:** Where do they spend time online? What are people complaining about in those spaces?

---

## 6. The "I Wish Someone Would Just..." Test

Ask the person:
- "Finish this sentence 10 times: 'I wish someone would just...'"
- "What do you do manually that you'd pay $50/month to automate?"
- "What's the last thing you Googled that had no good answer?"
- "What's something you've built for yourself that others ask you to share?"

---

## 7. Physical + Digital Combination Ideas

Products that combine physical and digital have high defensibility and perceived value.

Examples:
- Hardware device + companion app (fitness tracker, smart home, IoT)
- Physical product + digital community (buy the book, join the Discord)
- Physical goods + subscription digital content (meal kit + recipe platform)
- Service + software tool (consulting + proprietary tool)

Ask: "Is there a physical product in your space that would be 10x better with software attached?"

---

## 8. The Replication Model

Take something working in one context and apply it to another:
- **Category replication:** Uber for X, Airbnb for Y, LinkedIn for Z
- **Geography replication:** Take a successful model from one country to another
- **Audience replication:** Take a product that serves one audience and rebuild it for an underserved adjacent audience
- **Format replication:** Take content that works in one format (podcast) and repackage it for another (newsletter, course, community)

---

## Idea Quality Checklist

Before presenting an idea, run it through:
- [ ] Is the target customer *specific* enough that you could name 5 real people?
- [ ] Is there evidence people are already spending money on this problem (even on a bad solution)?
- [ ] Can the founder reach the first 10 customers without a marketing budget?
- [ ] Is there an unfair advantage tied to *this specific person*?
- [ ] What's the fastest possible path from zero to first dollar?
