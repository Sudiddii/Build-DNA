# Cofounder & Team Profiles

Use when building Section 6 of the Build Brief.

---

## The Four Cofounder Archetypes

Most founding teams need a combination of these. Identify which ones the founder already covers, then profile what's missing.

### 1. The Builder
What they do: Build the product — code, design, manufacture, create.
Signs you need one: Founder has the idea and the customers but can't build.
Where to find them: GitHub, Dribbble, hackathons, university engineering programs, developer communities.

### 2. The Seller
What they do: Talk to customers, close deals, generate leads, own distribution.
Signs you need one: Founder is technical but hates sales, avoids cold outreach, doesn't have the network.
Where to find them: LinkedIn (look for SDR/AE backgrounds), startup communities, people who've built audiences.

### 3. The Operator
What they do: Run the company — processes, hiring, finance, logistics, legal, keeping the trains running.
Signs you need one: Founder is a visionary who starts things but doesn't finish, hates admin, loses track of money.
Where to find them: Former startup ops hires, COO backgrounds, people who've scaled companies as early employees.

### 4. The Domain Expert
What they do: Bring deep industry credibility, customer relationships, regulatory knowledge, or technical depth.
Signs you need one: Founder is an outsider to the industry and needs a trusted insider.
Where to find them: Industry conferences, LinkedIn, alumni networks, former colleagues.

## Cofounder Fit Checklist

Before recommending a cofounder profile, check:

**Complementarity (required):**
- [ ] Different primary strength than the founder
- [ ] Different personality type (introvert/extrovert, detail/big-picture)
- [ ] Different network (different industries, geographies, or communities)

**Alignment (required):**
- [ ] Same vision for what the company could become
- [ ] Compatible risk tolerance and financial runway
- [ ] Compatible time commitment
- [ ] Shared values on how to treat customers, employees, and each other

**Red flags to flag:**
- Same background as the founder (two engineers, two marketers — who sells? who builds?)
- Friends only, no professional chemistry
- Unequal commitment but equal equity
- One person joins "once we get funding" — that's an advisor, not a cofounder

## Alternatives to a Cofounder

Sometimes the founder doesn't need a cofounder — they need a different kind of help:

| Gap | Alternative to Cofounder |
|---|---|
| Can't build | No-code tools (Webflow, Bubble, Shopify, Glide), vibe coding with AI, contract developer |
| Can't sell | Hire commission-only sales rep, productize so it sells itself, build content that attracts inbound |
| Can't operate | Virtual assistant, fractional COO, systems + automation |
| Need industry credibility | Advisor with equity (0.1–0.5%), strategic angel investor, advisory board |
| Need technical depth | Fractional CTO, technical advisor, strong developer contractor |

## Equity Ranges (Reference)

For calibration only — varies wildly by stage, market, and negotiation:

| Role | Stage | Typical Range |
|---|---|---|
| Cofounder | Pre-product | 20–50% |
| First engineer hire | Pre-seed | 1–3% |
| Advisor | Any | 0.1–0.5% |
| Fractional exec | Early | 0.25–1% |

Early equity should vest over 4 years with a 1-year cliff. Always use a vesting schedule.
